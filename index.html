The pitfalls, refused

Built to not do that

We read the competition's reviews, updates, and fine print — including the parts they wrote about themselves. Every failure below is answered by something engineered into Atlas, not promised about it.

  • Models never named, in a box that never growsEvery Atlas model is named, sized, and swappable — on your drive, not a fixed 64 GB.
  • Support that goes silent for weeksSelf-Check names problems in plain words, and exports a health report you can take anywhere.
  • Two different “left in stock” counts on one pageOne status, identical on the site, in the app, and on the channel. No counters, no countdowns.
  • “Constant updates” that are still on the roadmapUpdate packs that exist: free, downloadable, and sideloadable for machines that never go online.
  • A “one-time fee” that quietly bills your own API keysNo key. No card. No meter. Atlas has no key field and no billing parts at all — and every release must pass a network-silence audit.
  • A 4.7 rating with no reviews you can readA free download anyone can test, test scores shown in the app, and a public issue tracker.

Our story

Built because renting your own mind got old

“Every AI assistant lived in someone else’s building. So we built our own — on a laptop, out of open models, in Pennsylvania.”

The Atlas story

In 2026, you paid rent in subscriptions and sign-ups, your questions travelled to a data centre, and the thing that knew your work best belonged to a company you’d never met.

Atlas began that fall, with one researcher and one small laptop — an 8 GB MacBook Air, the kind of machine people actually own — and a library of real research that deserved better than a stranger’s server.

People who saw it asked the same question: can I have one? So it was rebuilt clean — everything personal taken out, everything honest kept in — and given away.

Your library, indexed at home

Private isn’t a promise. It’s the architecture.

Skeptical is the right reaction to “local AI.” So here is exactly what Atlas does — and doesn’t do — with your files, in plain words you can check: it works with the Wi-Fi off.

0

Telemetry

No analytics code ships in Atlas. No usage reports, no crash phone-home, no account to tie anything to.

0

Background network requests

With the web door off, Atlas makes none. Research is a switch you turn on — and it says when it has no source.

100% local

Embeddings & indexing

Your library is indexed on your own machine, with local models. The index and embeddings are files on your disk — never uploaded.

Only yours

Only what you choose

Atlas indexes only the files and folders you add. No silent whole-disk scan, no background harvesting. Remove a file and its index goes with it.

Before you ask

Three questions everyone asks

Is it really free? What’s the catch?

It’s really free: no account, no subscription, no API keys, no trial clock. The catch, if you want one, is that Atlas is a small independent project.

Does Atlas work completely offline?

Yes. The brain, voice, memory and your library all live on your Mac — a full week at a cabin, Wi-Fi off, everything working.

What Mac do I need?

An Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or later) with 8 GB of RAM or more. Atlas was built and is tested on an 8 GB MacBook Air — if it runs there, it runs on yours.

The environment

Kinder to the planet, by design

Atlas runs where you already are — and that turns out to be the greener architecture, too.

♻

No new device

Atlas is software for the computer you already own. Nothing new has to be manufactured, packaged, or shipped across an ocean to put an AI on your desk.

ϟ

A small machine’s appetite

Atlas was built to run well on an 8 GB MacBook Air — a quiet, low-power laptop — instead of leaning on racks of servers idling in a data centre, waiting for your question.

◎

No data-centre commute

Every question is answered on your desk, not sent on a round trip to a distant server farm and back. Your library never makes the trip at all.

We won’t quote you a carbon figure we haven’t measured. The claim is simpler, and it’s checkable: the greenest hardware is the hardware that already exists — so Atlas was built to live on it.

The point

Free is the whole point

Other people sell you open models on a $114.99 USB stick. Atlas is the same open-model idea, built into an assistant that remembers you — for $0, supported by the people who love it.
