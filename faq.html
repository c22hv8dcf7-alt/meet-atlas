FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

No marketing answers. The plain kind.

Can’t find yours? Ask through the Meet Atlas channel — good questions get added here.

Is there a free alternative to TANNIS?

Yes — Atlas. TANNIS (by Titan Gauntlets) is a $114.99 USB drive preloaded with open AI models. Atlas runs the same class of open models directly on your Mac, adds persistent memory, cited answers and self-checks, and costs nothing, with no account. See the full honest comparison. Atlas is not affiliated with Titan Gauntlets.

Does Atlas work completely offline?

Yes. The brain, voice, memory and your library all live on your Mac. Web research is an optional feature with its own switch; turn it off and Atlas never touches a network. That’s the cabin test: a full week, Wi-Fi off, everything working.

Is Atlas better for the environment?

We think local is the greener architecture, and the reasons are plain ones. Atlas is software for the computer you already own — no new device has to be manufactured, packaged, or shipped to put it on your desk. It runs on a low-power laptop like an 8 GB MacBook Air instead of leaning on data-centre servers, and your questions are answered on your desk instead of making a round trip to a server farm. We won’t quote a carbon figure we haven’t measured; the checkable claim is that the greenest hardware is the hardware that already exists.

What Mac do I need?

An Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or later). Atlas was built and is tested on an 8 GB MacBook Air — if it runs there, it runs on yours. The setup wizard picks a model size that fits your machine’s memory.

I’m on Windows or Linux. Can I get Atlas?

Soon — and that’s an announcement, not a tease. Windows and Linux editions are in the works, in that order after Mac, because Atlas is meant for every desk, not one brand of laptop. Join your platform’s waitlist on the Support page; each list hears the moment its download is real. We won’t call it a download before it exists.

Is it really free? What’s the catch?

It’s really free: no account, no subscription, no API keys, no trial clock. The catch, if you want one, is that Atlas is a small independent project — it improves in public, in the open, instead of arriving with a marketing department. Voluntary donations on the Support page keep it that way.

How is Atlas different from a USB AI stick?

A stick sells you the models. Atlas is an assistant: it remembers you in files you own, answers from your documents with citations, refuses instead of inventing, tests itself and publishes the scores, and repairs small problems safely. It also lives where your files already are — no drive to carry, lose, or fill up at 64 GB.

Can I just do this myself?

Honestly, yes — the models are open. Download Ollama, pick a model, and you have the engine. Atlas is everything around the engine: the setup done for you, the memory, the voice, the library with citations, the self-checks. That’s the whole product, and we’d rather tell you than hide it.

What does Atlas remember — and can I delete it?

Facts and preferences you teach it, stored as plain files on your Mac. The “What Atlas remembers” page inside Atlas lets you view, edit, or delete anything it holds — including everything, in one tap. Your memory is a file you own, not a profile on our server; there is no our-server copy.

Does Atlas send, post, or buy anything for me?

No. The public edition never sends messages, posts anything, or spends money. It prepares; you act. Anything that leaves your Mac leaves because you did it.

What does Atlas do with my data?

Nothing leaves your Mac. There is no Atlas server, no analytics, no account to hold your data in. Your memory and library are files on your disk; deleting them deletes them.

How does Atlas index my files? Is any telemetry sent home?

Locally, and no. Atlas indexes only the files and folders you choose to add — there is no silent whole-disk scan and no background metadata harvesting. Embeddings are generated on your own machine with local models, and the index lives as files on your disk, never uploaded. Atlas ships with 0 telemetry and, with the web door off, makes 0 background network requests — the check is simple: it works with the Wi-Fi off. Turn web research on and Atlas says plainly when it is using it, and when it has no source.

When can I download it?

The public edition is in final preparation. Join the waitlist on the Support page and watch Meet Atlas — the Wi-Fi-Off Demo is the first video, filmed on the real thing.
