Atlas vs TANNIS, honestly

Every fact sourced from their own page. Checked November 4, 2026.

TANNIS is a 64 GB USB stick with open AI models on it, sold by Titan Gauntlets. Atlas is the same open-model idea built into an assistant that lives on your computer — and it’s free. Every fact about TANNIS below comes from their own public product page, checked November 4, 2026 — the one campaign fact further down is marked as coming from public trackers. Where their page doesn’t advertise a feature, we say exactly that — not that it doesn’t exist.

$0 vs $114.99Atlas is a free download. TANNIS is a 64 GB drive, listed struck through from $169.99.
Memory & citations vs “not advertised”Atlas remembers you in files you own and cites its sources. Their page advertises neither (checked Nov 4, 2026).
Where TANNIS wins todayAll three platforms on sale now, and plug-and-play in 3–5 minutes. Atlas’s public editions are in preparation — and the gap is closing in public, on the board below.

Where they’re genuinely good — we mean this

They proved people want AI they own outright — their backers funded their Kickstarter in about 30 minutes (per public campaign trackers). Their plug-and-play drive is a real convenience, they support more platforms than we do today, and their page is honest that the DIY route exists. We just think the finished version of that idea should remember you, prove its answers — and be free.

Our opinion — labelled as opinion, because it is

In our opinion, $114.99 is a lot to charge for setup convenience over models anyone can download free — especially when the fast mode is labelled “lower intelligence” by the seller, the smart mode “takes the longest,” going online means paying a third party by the key, and the stock counter can’t agree with itself. Atlas is our answer: same local freedom, more assistant, no price tag.
