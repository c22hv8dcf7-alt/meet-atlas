Why a greeting

The assistant you’ve always imagined says hello first

Every assistant you’ve ever opened has waited, blank and silent, for you to know what to ask. The next Atlas will work the other way round: the welcome is the interface.

☀

Greets you, every open

By voice and on screen, the moment Atlas opens — warm, short, and by name where you’ve given one. No button to hunt for; opening is starting.

▦

Options, not homework

The greeting ends with work already offered: pick up where you left off, hear what matters today, run a guided job on your own files — or ignore the list and just talk.

✎

Knows how you like things

Your name, your voice, your way of working — preferences carried to every corner of Atlas automatically, from memory files you can read, edit, or delete. Never re-taught.

§

Briefs you, honestly

A short brief built only from what’s already on your computer — saved deadlines, follow-ups you asked for, updates waiting. If Atlas has no source for something, it says so. It never invents a busier day.

⚙

Works while you’re away

Leave a job with Atlas and it keeps moving — locally, while the machine is awake. When you come back, the greeting reports it: finished, needs your call, or paused, with the real reason.

◎

Your private server

All of it — the greeting, the memory, the work — stays on your own computer. The assistant who knows you best doesn’t live in someone else’s building, and can’t be taken away by a company’s bad quarter.

How an opening will go

Ninety seconds, no blank box

  • Open

    You open Atlas. The orb assembles. That’s your whole part in starting.

  • Greeted

    Atlas says hello — by voice and on screen — and if you left it work, it leads with the report: what finished, and the one thing that needs you.

  • Offered

    Work is put in front of you: pick up the last thread, hear today’s brief, or start a guided job on your own files. Tap one, say one, or say something entirely your own.

  • Working

    Atlas does the labour. You’re the grader, not the operator — results come back with their sources attached, ready for your verdict.

  • Leaving

    You leave whenever you like. No streak to break, no guilt on the way out. Atlas will simply be here, and will say so, next time you open the door.

What this isn’t: no “I missed you” chasing, no rewards for coming back, no made-up feelings. Atlas greets you when you open it — and only then. Welcome, not longing, is the design.

The point

Atlas today is a resident. Next, it’s a host.

Today’s Atlas already lives on your computer, remembers you in files you own, and answers with its sources attached. The greeting — coming to Atlas in a free update — is what will make all of that approachable: an assistant that meets you at the door, every time, and puts the work in front of you.
