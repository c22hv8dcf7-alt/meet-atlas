The Atlas Library

The shelf, all twenty of it.

Knowledge Packs are free bundles of real, sourced knowledge — an encyclopedia shelf, a school year's textbooks, a first-aid manual — that live on your machine and work completely offline. Download one once, and it answers forever, cited like everything else Atlas says.

Every pack is curated from named, openly licensed sources, spot-checked, and signed before Atlas will trust a single page — a public library, not the internet. Packs are data, never code: they can be read and cited, never obeyed. Delete one and the space is yours again.

Depth tiers

Take the shelf your disk can hold

As the shelf grows, a pack may offer three depths. They are alternatives, not additions — you install one depth per pack, and the picker shows the live storage total for your set before anything downloads.

▤

Essential

The core of the pack — what a household reaches for most — in the smallest file.

▦

Standard

The full pack as curated. The shelf's headline figure — about 200 MB for all twenty — describes the shelf at this depth.

◈

Comprehensive

Everything the sources offer, for the machine with room to spare. A depth is never offered if it would break the pack's size law.

Everyday Knowledge ships today in a single edition. Code & Computers already uses the pattern, as Core and Full. Depths arrive per pack, as each is offered — never as a surprise, and retrieval quality never depends on the depth you pick.

After the Twenty

The Maps shelf — your region, no signal required

When the twenty are complete, the shelf grows by vouched shelves only — and the first is offline maps: one file per region, downloaded once, then browsed and searched with no signal at all. A city runs to tens of megabytes; a state, up to about 2 GB — and the shelf states each region's real size before you download, the same honesty as everything else here.

Maps are never something Atlas cites. Answers stay cite-or-refuse over the text shelf; the maps simply give Atlas a where. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, published under the Open Database License (ODbL), and every map file carries that attribution inside it.

In development — the Maps shelf follows the Twenty.

The catalog

One list, read by everything

This shelf is published as a single machine-readable catalog — every downloadable file, its exact byte size, and its SHA-256: catalog.json. The picker, the app, and this page all read the same list, so what you see is what there is. When a file joins or leaves the shelf, the catalog is regenerated — it is part of the shelf's own checklist.
