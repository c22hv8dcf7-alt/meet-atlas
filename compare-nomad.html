The Comparison

Atlas vs Project NOMAD, honestly

Every fact sourced from their own pages and their public repository. Checked November 7, 2026.

Project NOMAD is the offline-library idea built as a home server: a free, open-source project by Crosstalk Solutions that puts Wikipedia, medical references, repair guides, maps and courses on a Linux box in your house, and serves them to every device you own — no internet required. Atlas wants the same thing for a different household. NOMAD put a library on a server in your closet. Atlas puts one in a window on the laptop you already own. Every fact about NOMAD below comes from their own site, FAQ and public repository, checked November 7, 2026 — including their own honest figures, quoted the way we’d want ours quoted.

Both free — a server vs a windowNOMAD is free and open source. Atlas is a free download. The difference isn’t price, and it isn’t offline — both are. It’s the machine, and the household it assumes.
Where NOMAD wins todayOne box serves the whole house. The full Wikipedia, maps and courses, available now. Some 42,500 people have starred it on GitHub. That is a real, mature project — and we say so.
Where Atlas is the on-rampA signed Mac app instead of a terminal. An AI proven on an 8 GB fanless laptop. A whole library of about 200 MB instead of 125 GB. For people who have a laptop, not a server room.

Where they’re genuinely good — we mean this

NOMAD is the offline idea done properly at server scale: honestly documented, carefully engineered, and free under a license that lets anyone build on it. Their update pipeline — scheduled windows, cool-off periods, pre-flight checks, even a dry-run command that rehearses an update before it happens — is the best engineering in either project, and we’ve adopted their Essential / Standard / Comprehensive depth tiers as the pattern for our own shelf, openly. If you have a spare Linux machine and a houseful of people to serve, NOMAD is a wonderful answer, and this page isn’t pretending otherwise.

Our opinion — labelled as opinion, because it is

In our opinion, the difference is the household each project imagines. NOMAD imagines one with a server closet, a terminal it isn’t afraid of, and 125 GB to spare. Atlas imagines the one with a laptop — possibly an old one, possibly a school Chromebook — and nobody who has ever typed sudo on purpose. Both households deserve their library to work with the Wi-Fi off. If yours is the first, take NOMAD with our admiration. If it’s the second, that’s the whole reason Atlas exists.
