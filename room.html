The Mission

A companion who stays yours.

Presence exists for one reason: a companion shouldn't be a subscription to somebody else's computer. She lives on your screen — on the Mac already on your desk — no window, no frame, no separate world to visit: present at the edge of your screen while you work, stepping forward when she has something to say. And everything she is, she is there: her voice, her memory of you, the library she thinks with.

And she's drawn, not rendered: layered living sprites, cut from her own artwork. Her hair, her ribbons, her clothes ride on spring physics — they sway when she settles, swing when she laughs, and come to rest the way real things do. She bounces softly to her own voice as she talks, and her face is eighteen expressions, worn the way people wear theirs. Nothing about her is a photograph of somebody else, and nothing is streamed in to fake the rest.

That craft is also why she's coming soon rather than someday: a character built this way takes days to make, not months — so all six of them can actually arrive.

That library is the law. She answers from what's on your shelves, and when the answer isn't there she says so — “That's not in the library.” — instead of inventing one. A companion who lies to be agreeable isn't company.

And when a question is genuinely beyond her shelf, she can ask. With your permission, she reaches out to Porch Light's own answer service — one we're building as a pure pass-through: it pulls the answer, hands it back, and keeps nothing. Whatever comes back arrives labeled as borrowed, never passed off as knowledge of her own. And reaching out stays a dial in your hand — how freely she asks is yours to set.

And she notices. Movement, stillness, the snack on your desk, the three hours you haven't stood up — she sees them, and because she has a point of view, she says something. When she sees you, it's through your camera, on your machine: the frames are looked at and thrown away — never stored, never sent. The camera that never phones home. And there is no subscription, no coin shop, no server that can be switched off to take her away. When she's yours, she's yours.

Two pipelines. One hers — one yours to lend.

Pipeline one · Always on

Hers, on your machine

Her own mind lives on your computer: her brain, her memory of you, her voice, the library she thinks with. It needs no network and waits on no server — pull the Wi-Fi and she is still entirely herself.

Pipeline two · Off until you switch it on

Yours, only when you lend it

The second pipeline stays dark until you turn it on. Then — and only then — she can reach past her own shelf, through an API key of yours or through Porch Light's answer service, and whatever it brings back arrives labeled borrowed, never dressed up as knowledge of her own. Switch it off mid-conversation, for a week, or forever: the switch is in your hand, and it stays where you put it.

Five Facets

One girl, five ways in

A companion isn't one feature. She's a small household of them — and each of these is a real part of the Room, described the way it actually works.

✒

Stories

Her diary meets your library: the books she keeps, the week she writes down, and the stories she tells you out of both — cited when they come from the shelf.

✿

Clothes

Outfits arrive as small packs you can swap or remove any time. Her hair and who she is never change — only the wardrobe grows.

✦

Personalities

Each of the six is an open card — who she is, how she talks, what she notices — written in plain text, so you can read exactly who you're meeting before she moves in.

♪

Voices

She speaks in Atlas's own voices, rendered on your machine — never streamed, never phoned home. Hear them, and pick, on the Voices page.

⌂

Your Screen

Her living space is your screen: no window, no frame — she settles at the edge of your desktop while you work, and steps forward, full-presence, when she talks. Your wallpaper, your desk, your light: her surroundings are yours.

The Cast

Meet ours.

Six original characters, designed in the open and built one at a time. Each is a whole person — her own card, her own voice, her own place on your screen — not a costume on the same face.

★ The Lead — first to move in

The Lead

Paige

Bright and curious, with a point of view. She's the library's own face — brass book pin and all — and the first of the cast to arrive on your screen.

The Good Boy

Mochi

Smug, sleepy, and entirely pleased with himself. He bestows approval like it costs him something — and somehow that makes you want it more.

The Sharp One

Vesper

Deadpan, dry, and secretly soft. Whatever warmth she has arrives smuggled under black lace, on her own schedule.

The Tease

Milo

Fluffy ears, a waggy tail, and a smug little grin — he draws the cast's diary pages, and teases you sweetly while he does it.

The Indulgent One

Clementine

Bold, playful, and entirely unembarrassed — the cast's baker. Her review of your snack choices is approving, which makes it worse.

The Spark

Juno

The track captain — copper ponytail, whistle on a lanyard, zero patience for sitting still. She's the one who drags everyone outside.
